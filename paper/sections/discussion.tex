\section{Discussion: what matters experimentally}\label{sec:discussion}

Ordering the effects studied by their numerical impact on the centre precision gives a clear
hierarchy.

\emph{1. The quality of the zero.} A filled zero is the largest effect by far. With the wrong
handedness the centre CRB at $L=50$\,nm rises from
\pnum{crb_center_vec_correct_L50_N100_nm}\src{crb_center_vec_correct_L50_N100_nm}\,nm to
\pnum{crb_center_vec_opposite_L50_N100_nm}\src{crb_center_vec_opposite_L50_N100_nm}\,nm, and
with linear polarization to
\pnum{crb_center_vec_linear_L50_N100_nm}\src{crb_center_vec_linear_L50_N100_nm}\,nm
(Table~\ref{tab:vectorial}). Even a small residual $\epsilon$ matters at small $L$: at $L=50$\,nm
a Gaussian pedestal of $\epsilon=0.05$ multiplies the CRB by
\pnum{crb_eps_degradation_L50_eps0p05_gaussian}\src{crb_eps_degradation_L50_eps0p05_gaussian}
(Table~\ref{tab:eps}). The practical consequence is that $L$ should not be reduced below
$L_{\mathrm{opt}}$ for the measured zero depth (Sec.~\ref{sec:nonideal}); polarization control
of the donut \cite{Lopez2023} comes before any other refinement.

\emph{2. Misalignment with a nominal model.} A displacement $\delta$ of the zeros (an error in
their positions, not a deformed donut) that is ignored
by the estimator produces a mean bias of
\pnum{misalignment_naive_bias_over_delta_pop_center_d2}\src{misalignment_naive_bias_over_delta_pop_center_d2}--\pnum{misalignment_naive_bias_over_delta_pop_center_d10}\src{misalignment_naive_bias_over_delta_pop_center_d10}$\,\delta$
at the centre ($\delta=2$--$10$\,nm, $L=100$\,nm) and larger at $(L/4,0)$
(\pnum{misalignment_naive_bias_over_delta_pop_Lq_d2}\src{misalignment_naive_bias_over_delta_pop_Lq_d2}--\pnum{misalignment_naive_bias_over_delta_pop_Lq_d10}\src{misalignment_naive_bias_over_delta_pop_Lq_d10}$\,\delta$). At $\delta=10$\,nm and $N=500$ the bias at the centre
(\pnum{misalignment_naive_bias_abs_center_d10_nm}\,$\pm$\,\pnumse{misalignment_naive_bias_abs_center_d10_nm}\src{misalignment_naive_bias_abs_center_d10_nm}\,nm)
is several times the statistical precision
(\pnum{misalignment_naive_sigma_center_d10_nm}\src{misalignment_naive_sigma_center_d10_nm}\,nm),
and it is invisible in the spread of repeated localizations. Calibrating the actual pattern and
using it in the likelihood removes it (honest MLE).

\emph{3. Background.} At $L=50$\,nm, $N=100$, $\sbr=10$ raises the centre CRB from
\pnum{crb_center_point_S27_L50_N100_nm}\src{crb_center_point_S27_L50_N100_nm}\,nm (point value)
to \pnum{crb_center_sbr10_L50_N100_nm}\src{crb_center_sbr10_L50_N100_nm}\,nm, and the photons
for $5$\,nm from \pnum{photons_for_5nm_L50_sbrinf}\src{photons_for_5nm_L50_sbrinf} to
\pnum{photons_for_5nm_L50_sbr10}\src{photons_for_5nm_L50_sbr10}. Background also regularizes the
model, making the MLE efficient at the centre. How the background is modelled matters little
for a single pattern (at the points compared, $x_0=25$ and $50$\,nm with $L=50$\,nm and
$N=100$, the largest change caused by a background constant per exposure is
$\pnum{bgphys_max_abs_change_bias_x_nm}\pm\pnumse{bgphys_max_abs_change_bias_x_nm}$\src{bgphys_max_abs_change_bias_x_nm}\,nm in the $x$ bias of the estimators of Sec.~\ref{sec:estimators} and
$(\pnum{bgphys_max_rel_change_sigma_over_crb_pct}\pm\pnumse{bgphys_max_rel_change_sigma_over_crb_pct})$\src{bgphys_max_rel_change_sigma_over_crb_pct}\% in their $\sigma/\crb$;
only the $x$ component of the bias was compared), but much for iterative MINFLUX: at constant power
the SBR falls approximately as $L^2$ and the final precision at $N_{\mathrm{tot}}=1000$ degrades to
\pnum{bgphys_iter_matchL150_sigma_nm}\src{bgphys_iter_matchL150_sigma_nm}\,nm
(Sec.~\ref{sec:iterative}). An ignored pedestal or a misjudged SBR biases the MLE by an amount
that does not vanish as $N$ grows (large-$N$ limit \pnum{naive_eps0p01_mle_bias_abs_x20_nm}\src{naive_eps0p01_mle_bias_abs_x20_nm}\,nm
at $x_0=20$\,nm for $\epsilon=0.01$), which exceeds the CRB above a few hundred photons for $\epsilon=0.01$ and above
$\sim10^4$ photons for $\epsilon=0.002$ (Sec.~\ref{sec:nonideal}).

\emph{4. Estimator and protocol.} The MLE is efficient at the centre with background, but not
uniformly inside the pattern at $N=100$
($\sigma/\crb=\pnum{mle_sbr10_sigma_over_crb_x15_N100}\src{mle_sbr10_sigma_over_crb_x15_N100}$
at $15$\,nm from the centre), and the linearized LMS estimators are strongly biased off-centre.
In iterative MINFLUX the search disk and the re-centring are part of the result; a heuristic
$L$ schedule based on the centre CRB leaves a fraction
\pnum{adaptive_frac_outside_next_radius}\,$\pm$\,\pnumse{adaptive_frac_outside_next_radius}\src{adaptive_frac_outside_next_radius} of emitters
outside the next pattern.

\emph{5. Definitions.} Without background the centre CRB depends on its definition by the factor
$\rho=\pnum{limit_to_point_ratio_L50}\src{limit_to_point_ratio_L50}$ at $L=50$\,nm; the
background-free MLE even beats both values. Benchmarks against ``the CRB at the centre'' should
state which one is used, or include background. Numerical CRBs regularized at the zero, such as
that of SimuFLUX \cite{MarinRies2026}, return the point value\src{lit_simuflux_crb_point_value}:
for their default donut ($\fwhm=310$\,nm) and a TCP with $L=75$\,nm, $N=100$, the point value is
\pnum{simuflux_tcp_crb_point_L75_fwhm310_nm}\src{simuflux_tcp_crb_point_L75_fwhm310_nm}\,nm and
the limit \pnum{simuflux_tcp_crb_limit_L75_fwhm310_nm}\src{simuflux_tcp_crb_limit_L75_fwhm310_nm}\,nm.
Two further conventions must be converted before comparing numbers: in the public SimuFLUX
implementation the donut has the power of a unit-peak Gaussian, so its ring peak is $1/e$ and its
zero offset corresponds to $\epsilon=e\times$ offset in our normalization (ring peak $1$)\src{conv_eps_normalization};
and SimuFLUX reports errors along one axis ($x$), whereas our $\sigma$ and rmse average the two
axes (Eq.~\eqref{eq:crb}), which differ off-centre.

\emph{6. Beam model.} Once the zero is correct, the scalar LG model is adequate if its size
parameter is chosen to match the curvature of the actual zero: the error in the centre CRB is
then below the percent level (Table~\ref{tab:vectorial}). With the default $\fwhm$ the
discrepancy grows with $L$, reaching a CRB ratio of
\pnum{crb_vec_over_lg300_L150_N100}\src{crb_vec_over_lg300_L150_N100} at $L=150$\,nm.

Iterative MINFLUX reaches an error
\pnum{iterative_ratio_min}\src{iterative_ratio_min}--\pnum{iterative_ratio_max}\src{iterative_ratio_max}
times that of the ideal camera for the same photons in our idealized setting; the zero depth,
the misalignment and, except for single runs at $N_{\mathrm{tot}}=1000$ (with $\sbr=10$,
\pnum{iterative_sbr10_sigma_nm}\,$\pm$\,\pnumse{iterative_sbr10_sigma_nm}\src{iterative_sbr10_sigma_nm}\,nm,
and with a background constant per exposure), the background were absent from that simulation;
they set how much of this advantage survives in an experiment.
