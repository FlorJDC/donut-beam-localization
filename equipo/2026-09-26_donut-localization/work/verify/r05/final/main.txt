Localization of single emitters with donut beams:
a reproducible simulation study of MINFLUX-type patterns
flor-choque∗
We present a reproducible simulation study of the localization of single fluorescent emitters with
donut-shaped excitation beams in the triangular coordinate pattern (TCP) of MINFLUX: three
donut zeros on a circle of diameter L plus a central exposure. Every computed number is produced
by a script and was reproduced by an independent verifier. (i) At the TCP centre the Cramér–Rao
bound (CRB) is discontinuous without background: the r →0 limit lies below the point value
at r = 0 (Balzarotti Eq. S27), with ratio 2/
√
5 for a quadratic zero; for L = 50 nm, N = 100
photons and a donut size parameter fwhm = 300 nm the limit is 1.605 nm against 1.802 nm; any
finite signal-to-background ratio (SBR) removes the discontinuity. (ii) A high-numerical-aperture
vectorial vortex donut of the correct handedness is reproduced by a scalar Laguerre–Gauss donut
with matched zero curvature (CRB ratio 0.9962 at L = 150 nm), whereas the wrong handedness fills
the zero to 0.8452 of the ring maximum. (iii) With SBR = 10 the maximum-likelihood estimator
(MLE) is eﬀicient at the centre (L = 50 nm, N = 100;σ/σCRB = 0.991±0.005); without background
it is biased and supereﬀicient. (iv) Iterative MINFLUX with 1000 photons and our protocol (L
reduced from 150 to 25 nm, no background) reaches an error of 0.508 nm, 0.1606 times that of an
ideal camera. (v) A finite zero depth ϵ sets an optimal L ∝fwhm√ϵ, and a misaligned pattern
analysed with the nominal model produces, at the centre, a mean bias of 0.747–0.783 times the
displacement δ of the zeros (δ = 2–10 nm, L = 100 nm, SBR = 10), which an estimator using the
true pattern removes.
I.
INTRODUCTION
MINFLUX localizes a single emitter by probing it se-
quentially with an excitation intensity minimum placed
at a few known positions and comparing the detected
photon counts [1]. Because the fluorescence vanishes near
the zero, the information per detected photon is large and
the localization precision scales with the size L of the
probing pattern rather than with the diffraction-limited
width of a point-spread function; nanometre precision is
reached with a few hundred photons, in two and three
dimensions [1, 2].
The same probabilistic framework
covers other sequential structured-illumination schemes
[3, 4], multiphoton excitation with light minima [5] and
nanoscale tracking [6]. The excitation minimum is usu-
ally a toroidal (donut) focus produced by a vortex phase
mask; its zero depends on polarization and handedness
[7, 8].
Theoretical analyses of MINFLUX, starting with the
Supplementary Material of Ref. [1], use the scalar parax-
ial Laguerre–Gauss (LG) donut, a perfect zero, an exact
knowledge of the pattern and the closed-form CRB at
the pattern centre. Here we ask, with simulations whose
every number is traceable to a script, how far these ide-
alizations can be pushed. We address five questions:
R1 Beam model. How much does the scalar LG donut
differ from the vectorial high-NA donut near the
zero, and what does it change in the CRB? What
happens with the wrong handedness or with linear
polarization?

The main results are: a precise statement of the dis-
continuity of the centre CRB and of the two possible
definitions (Sec. IV, App. A); a quantitative equivalence
between the vectorial donut and a curvature-matched LG
donut, and the collapse of precision with a filled zero
(Sec. III); the eﬀiciency and the failure modes of the es-
timators (Sec. V); the photon scaling of iterative MIN-
FLUX against an ideal camera (Secs. VI and VII); and
the optimal L for a finite zero depth and the bias caused
by misalignment (Sec. VIII). Section IX ranks these ef-
fects by their numerical impact, and Sec. X lists what
remains open.

V.
ESTIMATORS VERSUS THE CRB (R3)
All MC results in this section use L = 50 nm, fwhm =
300 nm, seed 42 and 104 repetitions unless stated other-
wise; standard errors (SE) of σ are nonparametric boot-
strap estimates.
MLE with background: eﬀicient at the centre. With
SBR = 10 the model is regular at the centre (p3 > 0).
For N = 100 and an MLE search disk of radius L, the
MLE precision is σ = 1.943±0.010 nm against the bound
1.960 nm of Eq. (8): an eﬀiciency σ/σCRB = 0.991±0.005.
This is the value we define as the MLE eﬀiciency at the
centre; the choice SBR = 10 (a regular, physical case) is
a declared convention (Sec. X).
MLE without background:
biased and supereﬀicient.
Without background p3 ∝r2, the model is not regu-
lar at the centre, and the MLE beats the CRB limit:
σ = 1.348 ± 0.007 nm, i.e. σ/σlim = 0.840 ± 0.004 and
σ/σpt = 0.748 ± 0.004 at N = 100. The ratio does not
approach one as N grows [Fig. 5(c)], so this is not a small-
N effect. The price is a bias, toward the centre close to
it [for x0 ≲8 nm, Fig. 5(d)]: at r = (2, 0) nm the mean
error along x is −0.344 ± 0.008 nm (4 × 104 repetitions)
[Fig. 5(d)]. The CRB bounds only unbiased estimators
of a regular model, so there is no contradiction; it means
that, without background, neither the point value nor
the limit is a bound on what an MLE reports near the
centre.

A.
Finite zero depth
Experimental donuts have a residual intensity ϵ at
the zero, relative to the ring maximum (below 0.2% in
Ref. [1]).
We model it in two ways (App. A): a con-
stant pedestal, I = ILG + ϵ, and a Gaussian pedestal,
I = (eaρ2 + ϵ)e−aρ2.
The constant pedestal is ex-
actly a background: at the centre the CRB is Eq. (8)
with SBRϵ = 3e x e−x/(4ϵ); the Gaussian pedestal is not
equivalent to any background, because it differs between
exposures and reduces the slope of the donut. With ϵ > 0
the point value equals the limit and tends, as ϵ →0, to
the point value σpt, not to σlim.
Degradation at fixed L. Table II lists σCRB(ϵ)/σpt at
the centre. The loss is largest for small L, since SBRϵ ∝
x ∝L2: at L = 50 nm a pedestal of 5% roughly doubles
the CRB. The two pedestal models agree to first order in
ϵ up to corrections of relative order x2.

IX.
DISCUSSION: WHAT MATTERS
EXPERIMENTALLY
Ordering the effects studied by their numerical impact
on the centre precision gives a clear hierarchy.
1. The quality of the zero. A filled zero is the largest ef-
fect by far. With the wrong handedness the centre CRB
at L = 50 nm rises from 1.600 nm to 147.9 nm, and with
linear polarization to 60.77 nm (Table I). Even a small
residual ϵ matters at small L: at L = 50 nm a Gaus-
sian pedestal of ϵ = 0.05 multiplies the CRB by 2.152
(Table II). The practical consequence is that L should
not be reduced below Lopt for the measured zero depth
(Sec. VIII); polarization control of the donut [7] comes
before any other refinement.
2. Misalignment with a nominal model. A displace-
ment δ of the zeros that is ignored by the estimator
produces a mean bias of 0.747–0.783 δ at the centre
(δ = 2–10 nm, L = 100 nm) and larger at (L/4, 0) (0.802–
0.863 δ). At δ = 10 nm and N = 500 the bias at the cen-
tre (7.71 ± 0.16 nm) is several times the statistical pre-
cision (2.015 nm), and it is invisible in the spread of re-
peated localizations. Calibrating the actual pattern and
using it in the likelihood removes it (honest MLE).
3. Background. At L = 50 nm, N = 100, SBR = 10
raises the centre CRB from 1.802 nm (point value) to
1.960 nm, and the photons for 5 nm from 13.00 to 15.37.
Background also regularizes the model, making the MLE
eﬀicient at the centre.
4.
Estimator and protocol. The MLE is eﬀicient at
the centre with background, but not uniformly inside the
pattern at N = 100 (σ/σCRB = 1.51 at 15 nm from the
centre), and the linearized LMS estimators are strongly
biased off-centre. In iterative MINFLUX the search disk
and the re-centring are part of the result; a heuristic
L schedule based on the centre CRB leaves a fraction
0.0265 ± 0.0016 of emitters outside the next pattern.
5. Definitions. Without background the centre CRB
depends on its definition by the factor ρ = 0.8905 at
L = 50 nm; the background-free MLE even beats both
values.
Benchmarks against “the CRB at the centre”
should state which one is used, or include background.
6. Beam model. Once the zero is correct, the scalar LG
model is adequate if its size parameter is chosen to match
the curvature of the actual zero: the error in the centre
CRB is then below the percent level (Table I). With the
default fwhm the discrepancy grows with L, reaching a
CRB ratio of 0.9717 at L = 150 nm.
Iterative MINFLUX reaches an error 0.1577–0.1831
times that of the ideal camera for the same photons in
our idealized setting; the zero depth, the misalignment
and (except for one run with SBR = 10, which gives
0.616 ± 0.003 nm at Ntot = 1000) the background were
absent from that simulation, and they set how much of
this advantage survives in an experiment.
X.
LIMITATIONS AND OPEN POINTS
The following points are either not verified or rest on
declared choices; they are listed here rather than stated
as results.
• Scope of the model.
All simulations are two-
dimensional, with a fixed N (multinomial statis-
tics), a background equal in all exposures with the
SBR held fixed at every position (Sec. II), no dipole
orientation effects, no aberrations beyond the zero
depth and no drift. The camera reference is ideal
(Sec. VI); real cameras are worse.
• rmse normalization. We use the per-axis conven-
tion of Eq. (5) for σ and rmse. Whether it coincides
with the error definition of Masullo et al. [3] was
not checked, because the main text of that work is
not in our notes.
• SBR = 10 for the MLE eﬀiciency. The eﬀiciency
at the centre is defined with SBR = 10; this was
chosen by the study coordinator on behalf of the
author because without background the model is
not regular (Sec. V). It is a convention, pending
explicit confirmation by the author.
• Vectorial ring diameter versus PyFocus. Our peak-
to-peak diameters of the vectorial donut as a func-
tion of the filling factor (n = 1.5) agree to within
a few percent with the values we transcribed from
Fig. 10 of Ref. [8] at filling factors 0.5, 2 and uni-
form illumination, but differ clearly at filling factor
1. We do not claim agreement there; an indepen-
dent comparison is pending.
• Heavy tail at low photon numbers. In iterative MIN-
FLUX at Ntot = 250 the error distribution is heavy-
tailed and the bootstrap SE of σ is itself unstable;
the full-range slope inherits this.
• Range dependence of the L exponent. The exponent
1.004 applies only to L ≪fwhm; it increases when
the fit range extends to larger L.
• Three-dimensional donuts and axial misalignment
are outside the scope of this study.
